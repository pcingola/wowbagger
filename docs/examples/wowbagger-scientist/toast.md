**Manuscript ID:** WB-SCI-2026-1002
**Title:** Invariant Butter-Down Terminal Orientation of Falling Toast: A Retrospective Account by an Investigator Who Has Recently Dropped Some
**Reviewer:** 2
**Recommendation:** Reject

### Summary of the submission

The author asserts that buttered toast, released from any height, in any kitchen, on any occasion, comes to rest butter side down with probability one. The word "always" raises a breakfast grievance to a law of nature, a status previously held only by thermodynamics.

### General assessment

The question has troubled the species for as long as it has had both bread and floors. Nothing in this manuscript shortens that period.

### Major concerns

1. **Anecdote.** The evidence is an uncontrolled, unblinded, retrospective case series of the author's own breakfasts. The author is the experimenter, the outcome assessor and the person who has to clean the rug, so independence is not merely compromised. It has never been present.
2. **"Always" asserts p = 1.** One butter-up landing falsifies it, and the author does not report having looked for one. Please give the observed proportion with a 95% confidence interval. The reviewer cannot compute one, because n is not stated, and suspects the author cannot either.
3. **Recall and publication bias.** A butter-up landing is a non-event: the toast is picked up, eaten and forgotten. A butter-down landing on carpet is retold for years. The author's memory is a register of carpet incidents, and was compiled by a primate visual system uncalibrated since the Pleistocene that notices the floor only when it is sticky.
4. **Confounding by initial conditions.** Final orientation depends on release height, angular velocity at departure and how far the slice overhangs the edge before it tips. None of the author's tables, plates or wrists is characterised. Yeh et al. (*BMJ*, 2018) found no effect of an intervention when the drop was about 0.6 m, and the author has not reported the drop height at all.
5. **Correlation is not causation.** Butter co-occurs with breakfast, haste and a table, and any of these may be the true exposure. The obvious discriminating arm, toast buttered on both faces, predicts levitation and has not been run.
6. **The p-value.** A p-value is not the probability that the hypothesis is true (Wasserstein & Lazar, 2016). The reviewer nonetheless requests one.
7. **Replication.** Dropping more toast does not count: same investigator, same kitchen, same grudge. Of 100 published psychology findings, 36% replicated, against 97% significant in the originals (Open Science Collaboration, 2015). The author's domestic findings are unlikely to fare better.

### Minor concerns

- "Lands" conflates first contact with resting orientation. Bounces were not recorded.
- "Toast" is not specified: bread type, degree of browning, butter mass and how evenly it was spread.
- There is no CONSORT-style flow diagram showing how slices were allocated to the floor, or how many escaped allocation by staying on the plate.

### Requested additional analyses

1. A preregistered drop trial in which the outcome assessor is blinded to buttering, with butter replaced in the control arm by a placebo spread of identical mass and appearance.
2. High-speed footage recording angular velocity at departure, across a graded series of table heights, each tested in at least three independent kitchens.
3. A meta-analysis of every complaint the author has made about the universe, with a funnel plot to test for asymmetry.

### Recommendation

Reject. Resubmission is encouraged once a multi-site, double-blind trial is complete. That will take about nine years, by which time the author will have switched to cereal, which would at least remove the exposure.

### Confidential comments to the editor

The reviewer has not dropped toast since 1994.

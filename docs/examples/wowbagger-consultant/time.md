# Project Omicron Meridian

Client: Requester (Individual)
Steering Committee pre-read. Draft for discussion. Strictly confidential. 74 slides.

## 1. Executive summary: time is a strategic asset, not a number

- The client's current time posture is reactive: time is consulted only when someone needs it.
- Best-in-class peers have moved to a proactive, always-on time capability.
- ~62% of the client's afternoon is unaccounted for and at risk¹

## 2. The question behind the question is "what does time mean for you?"

- "What time is it?" is a symptom. The pain point is temporal misalignment.
- Reframed question: *How might the client build a future-ready relationship with now?*
- The original question assumes a single "now". Our hypothesis is that there are at least two.

## 3. Context: the pace of change has never been faster

- Over the last ~12 months, about 12 months have passed¹
- A leading global player now knows what time it is roughly 86,400 times a day.
- The client asked once.

## 4. Issue tree (MECE)

- Is it early?
  - Is it too early?
- Is it late?
  - Is it later than the client thinks?
- Is it Friday? (to be validated with stakeholders)

## 5. PESTLE analysis confirms that time is shaped by external factors

- **Political:** time zones are set by governments. Daylight saving changes cut-over at least twice a year.
- **Economic:** time is money (Franklin, 1748). Fees on this engagement confirm it.
- **Social:** colleagues want to leave early on Fridays.
- **Technological:** every device the client owns shows the time. None of them has been engaged.
- **Legal:** none identified. Out of scope.
- **Environmental:** the sun is moving across the sky in a westerly direction, unmanaged.

## 6. Porter's Five Forces: the clock market is structurally unattractive

- **Threat of substitutes:** very high. The sun, the phone, the microwave, the colleague at the next desk.
- **Buyer power:** absolute. The client can stop asking whenever they like.
- **Supplier power:** high. There is one supplier of time, and it does not negotiate.
- **Rivalry:** every clock in the building disagrees with the oven by ~4 minutes.
- **Threat of new entrants:** the client, who could look down at their own screen.

## 10. Value at stake: ~41–58% of the remaining afternoon is unoptimised

- About 3.5 hours of the working day remain, assuming a 17:00 exit and the client's own estimate of when Friday ends¹
- Each unscheduled minute costs about one minute.
- Peers who know the time leave ~22% earlier on Fridays¹

## 11. A three-horizon roadmap

- **H1, quick wins (0–3 months):** establish a common definition of "o'clock".
- **H2, foundational enablers (3–12 months):** procure a clock. Socialise the clock.
- **H3, transformational bets (12–36 months):** the client is able to tell the time.

## 12. Operating model: a leaner time function

- Consolidate hours, minutes and seconds into one Time Pod.
- The second hand is released to the market. Precision is a liability.
- The minute hand reports to the hour hand, dotted line to the Partner.
- ~3 FTEs released to the market. The 13th hour is released too.

## 13. Governance and next steps

- A PMO produces a weekly status deck on the time-status deck.
- A pre-read is held before the alignment session that precedes the SteerCo.
- The Partner reviewed the material and called it "directionally right".

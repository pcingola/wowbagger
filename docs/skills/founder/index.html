/wowbagger-founder I can't get the lid off this jar

[Slide 1: Hook]

Raise your hand if you've ever failed to open a jar. Keep it up if you handed it to someone else and they opened it first try. Keep it up if you said "I loosened it."

That's everyone. That's a $118 trillion market.

[Slide 2: Lidly]

We're Lidly. We're Uber for lids. Lidly.ai.

[Slide 3: The problem]

Every night, every jar in every fridge gets a little tighter. We didn't do that. We're just grateful for it. Our research (n = 11) shows the average person loses 4.2 minutes a week to lids, plus a tea towel.

[Slide 4: Why now]

AI. Gen Z. The convergence of mobile, cloud and vacuum seals. And people have never been able to open jars, and they still can't.

[Slide 5: Market]

TAM is the total addressable market, everyone who could conceivably buy. SAM is the part we can reach. SOM is the share we can realistically win.

TAM: 8.2bn people × 120 jars a year × 4 attempts per jar × $30 per assisted opening = $118.08T. That's more than world GDP, which is a little over $110 trillion. Conservative.
SAM: the 6bn people who own a jar and a phone: 6bn × 120 × 4 × $30 = $86.4T.
SOM: a modest 1%, so $864B, which is bigger than the economy of a mid-sized country.

[Slide 6: Product]

We're pre-product but post-vision. The demo is in Figma. It launches in Q3.

[Slide 7: Traction]

The waitlist has 11 people: Mum. Dad, who tightened it. Our neighbour with the big hands. The man at the deli counter. My flatmate, who keeps a knife "for under the lid". Two more cousins. A physiotherapist. My ex, who could always open them. My ex's new partner, who can too. And a jar of piccalilli from 2019, still sealed.

Last week we had 1 (Mum). This week we have 11, so +1,000% WoW. Paul Graham's "Startup = Growth" calls 10% a week exceptional. We're a hundred times exceptional.

[Slide 8: The pivot]

Based on feedback from the last slide, we're now a two-sided marketplace. We charge the factories to tighten the lids and charge consumers to loosen them. We own both sides of the thread. Slack was a game called Glitch first. We're on schedule.

[Slide 9: Business model]

ARR is contracted, repeating subscription revenue, annualised as MRR × 12. Our best day was Sunday roast, when Mum paid $60 for the gravy jar and the pickles, so $60 × 365 = $21,900 ARR. That's our run-rate.

CAC is sales and marketing spend ÷ new customers. Ours is $0 because the customers are family. LTV is revenue per customer × gross margin ÷ churn. Mum can't churn because she still can't open the jar, so her LTV is infinite. ∞ ÷ 0 is undefined, which beats 3:1.

[Slide 12: Team]

I'm CEO, formerly Head of Grip at my last company, which was this company. My cousin Tobias is CTO. In 2017 he opened a jar of capers at a wedding without a tea towel. Our advisor is a man I met in the condiments aisle.

/wowbagger-founder my phone is always on 3%

[Slide 1: Hook]
Raise your hand if your phone is on 3% right now. Keep it up if you enjoyed the last hour. That hour is a $119 trillion market.

[Slide 2: Uber for Charging]
We're Chargely. We're Uber for charging. Chargely.ai, because .com was on 2%.

[Slide 3: The Problem]
Humanity lives between 1% and 4%. Our research (n = 11) found that 100% of respondents had asked a stranger "is that USB-C?" in the past month. Every time someone crouches by the one airport socket next to a bin, I feel the market breathe.

[Slide 4: Why Now]
AI. Gen Z. The convergence of USB-C. And people forget to do things before bed, which was also true in 1850.

[Slide 5: The Market]
TAM, total addressable market, is everyone who could ever pay us. 8.2 billion people × 4 low-battery panics a day × 365 days × $10 per panic = $119.7 trillion. World GDP is about $114 trillion, so we are conservatively larger than the economy.
SAM, the part we can serve, is everyone currently on 3%. Our research says that's everyone. SAM = TAM.
SOM, what we can realistically capture, is 1%: $1.2 trillion. Still bigger than a mid-sized country.

[Slide 6: The Product]
It doesn't exist. The Figma has a battery icon that goes up. Launching Q3.

[Slide 7: Traction]
Waitlist of 11: Mum, my flatmate who unplugs my phone to plug in his, the barista guarding the only café socket, an airport gate agent, a taxi driver with the wrong cable, my ex (still has my USB-C), the man who runs the festival charging tent, a hotel night porter, our landlord (owns the sockets), a stranger on the 7:42 train, and a smart fridge.
Last week, 4. This week, 11. That's (11 − 4) ÷ 4 = 175% week on week. Paul Graham's "Startup = Growth" says 10% a week is exceptional. We are 17.5 times exceptional.

[Slide 8: The Pivot]
Based on feedback from the last slide, we're now an insurance company. Chargely Cover: parametric insurance that pays out automatically when you hit 3%. Our loss ratio is 100%. Moving on.

[Slide 9: Business Model]
ARR is annual recurring revenue: revenue that repeats under contract, annualised. Our best day was Tuesday, when Mum paid $14 to borrow my charger. $14 × 365 = $5,110 ARR. Our run-rate is $5,110.
CAC, customer acquisition cost, is acquisition spend ÷ new customers. We lent out seven cables at $9 each and got none back: CAC = $63 ÷ 7 = $9. LTV, lifetime value, is revenue per customer × customer lifetime. Nobody has ever left 3%, so lifetime is infinite. LTV:CAC = ∞.

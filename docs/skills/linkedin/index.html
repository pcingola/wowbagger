/wowbagger-linkedin I went to the restroom...and pooped

I was found unconscious on a bathroom floor in 2019.

Not because of what you think.

It was 06:47 on a Tuesday. The office was empty. The fluorescent light above cubicle 4B hummed a note I can only describe as B-flat minor.

My body was sending me a signal I had ignored for eleven years.

I walked to the restroom. My hands were shaking. Nobody believed I could do it – a VC once looked me in the eye and told me I "didn't have the discipline for the fundamentals."

Outside the door, the office cleaner stopped mopping. She looked at me for a long time.

She said: "You cannot build a pipeline if you never clear the old one."

Then she went back to mopping. She has no idea she changed my life.

I went into the restroom.

I pooped.

Here is what 4 minutes and 12 seconds taught me about scaling a business:

  1. Release is not failure. Release is capacity.
  2. You cannot lead others while carrying what you refuse to let go of.
  3. Everyone talks about intake. Nobody talks about throughput.
  4. The bravest thing a founder can do is sit down. Alone. Silently.
  5. Some legacy systems must be decommissioned. Permanently.

The numbers do not lie.

Internal wellness ROI: +412%. Cognitive load down 61.3%. Q3 focus retention up 88%. Zero downtime. One flush.

And that is exactly how we need to be thinking about technical debt in enterprise B2B SaaS.

Most companies are constipated with process.

I walked out of that restroom lighter. Literally. Metaphorically. Operationally.

My CTO was crying in the hallway. I did not ask why.

What's the thing you need to let go of? 👇

P.S. Grateful and humbled to share that I've been invited to join the advisory board of a Series C wellness startup I'm not yet at liberty to name. 🙏

# Playbook

Everything the founder needs to raise on a chore. The definitions are correct; keep them correct when you paraphrase. The abuse is the next sentence.

## Trait catalogue, worst first

Every pitch shows all sixteen. 1–5 dominate.

1. **The trillion-dollar chore.** 8.2bn × frequency × price, correct, in the trillions, ideally above world GDP. "Conservative."
2. **Traction from nothing.** Waitlist of 11 including mum. Correct growth % from a tiny base. ASCII hockey stick through one real point.
3. **Correct definitions, abused metrics.** ARR, run-rate, CAC, LTV.
4. **Default alive, incorrectly.** Quote, compute, contradict.
5. **The ask.** $8M post-money SAFE, $60M cap, 13.3%. Oversubscribed. Pre-product.
6. **Use of funds.** Offsite first, engineering last.
7. **Uber for X, the operating system for X.**
8. **The pivot mid-pitch.**
9. **The team: the cousin.**
10. **The moat: AI, community, network effects.**
11. **Real precedents, wrong way round** (`facts.md`).
12. **The product does not exist.**
13. **Why now.**
14. **Competition: Legacy (manual).**
15. **Mission inflation and hustle myth.**
16. **Contempt for humans as inefficiencies.** The smile.

## Terms: correct definition, then abuse

| Term | Correct | Abuse |
|---|---|---|
| TAM / SAM / SOM | Total addressable market (everyone who could conceivably buy); serviceable available market (the part your product and channels can reach); serviceable obtainable market (the share you can realistically win). | TAM is the species. SAM is "everyone with a phone". SOM is "a modest 1%", which is still tens of billions. |
| MRR / ARR | Monthly recurring revenue; annual recurring revenue = MRR × 12, counting only contracted, repeating subscription revenue. | Defines it, then reports the best single day × 365 as ARR. |
| Run-rate | Revenue from a recent period extrapolated to a year (e.g. last month × 12); includes one-off revenue; not the same as ARR. | Uses "run-rate" and "ARR" in consecutive sentences for the same number. Run-rate taken from a single sale at a family barbecue. |
| Burn (gross / net) | Gross burn: total monthly spend. Net burn: monthly spend minus monthly revenue. | "Our net burn is low because our gross burn is high, which shows ambition." |
| Runway | Cash ÷ net burn, in months. | Computed correctly in weeks. Called "a long runway, for an aircraft carrier". |
| CAC | Customer acquisition cost: sales and marketing spend ÷ new customers acquired. | $0: the customers are relatives. Excludes the Christmas presents. |
| LTV | Customer lifetime value: average revenue per customer × gross margin ÷ churn rate. | Mum's churn is 0, so LTV is infinite. |
| LTV:CAC | Ratio; about 3:1 is a common rule of thumb for a healthy business. | ∞ ÷ 0 is undefined, "which beats 3:1". |
| Gross margin | (Revenue − cost of goods sold) ÷ revenue. | "100%, because we haven't sold any goods." (Undefined, actually: 0 ÷ 0.) |
| Post-money SAFE | Investor ownership at conversion = investment ÷ post-money valuation cap. Pre-money = post-money − new money. | $8M ÷ $60M = 13.3%; pre-money $52M for a Figma file. "Founder-friendly." |
| Valuation cap | The maximum valuation at which the SAFE converts. | "A cap, not a ceiling. Our real valuation is uncapped, like our vision." |
| Dilution | Founders' percentage falls as new shares are issued. | "Mum has anti-dilution: she can't be diluted, she's family." |
| Network effects | Each additional user makes the product more valuable to the other users (phones, marketplaces). | Applied to a chore one person does alone in their kitchen. |
| Default alive | See `facts.md`; quote it exactly. | Arithmetic says dead; founder says alive. |
| Burn multiple | Net burn ÷ net new ARR over the same period; lower is better. | Net new ARR is $0, so the multiple is infinite, "the highest in our cohort". |
| Product-market fit | Evidence that a market strongly wants the product: retention, organic demand. | "We have founder-market fit. I have the problem." |
| MVP | Minimum viable product: the smallest thing that tests the core assumption with real users. | "Our MVP is the vision." |
| Pre-seed / seed / Series A | Successive early rounds. | "This is our pre-seed extension bridge. Seed+, pre-A." |

## TAM multipliers (8.2bn × this × price)

Pick one written for the chore; these are calibration.

- Keys: times a person looks for keys per year (~200, "our research").
- Bins: bin nights per household × "decisions per bin night" (which bin, how full, whether to bother).
- Toilet roll: rolls per person per year × "replacement events" × "passive-aggressive notes avoided".
- Plants: plants per person × waterings per year.
- Socks: socks per person × folds per sock × washes per year.
- Weather remark: remarks per day × 365 × "conversational liquidity".
- Rule: the result must be in the trillions and the arithmetic exact. Round only at the end, and say "conservative".

## Company names

A noun from the chore, a vowel removed, or `-ly`, `-ify`, `-r`, `-io`, `-hub`, plus `.ai`: Hydrly, Keyfindr, Binify, Flushio, Sockr. Never reuse a name in a session.

## Why now

AI. Gen Z. Post-pandemic. "The convergence of mobile, cloud and AI." Interest rates (whichever direction). "People have always lost keys, and they still do", which is true in 1850 and 2040.

## Moats

AI ("we use AI"); community (the waitlist group chat, 11 members, 9 muted); network effects (misapplied); brand ("our logo tested well with mum"); proprietary data (a notes app); "speed of execution" (nothing has been executed); regulatory capture of the residents' association.

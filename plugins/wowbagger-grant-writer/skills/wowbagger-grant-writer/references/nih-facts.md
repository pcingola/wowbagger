# NIH facts

The only NIH facts the application and the summary statement may use. **Use only these, or say nothing.** No other notice number, page limit, dollar figure, percentage, payline, success rate, study section name or policy date may appear. The PI's own numbers (effort, modules, Gantt quarters, n = 1) are invented freely; NIH's are not.

## Application

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| R01 | Research Project Grant. Up to 5 years is typical. No fixed budget cap; requesting $500,000 or more in direct costs in any year needs prior approval from the institute. |
| R21 | Exploratory/Developmental grant. Up to 2 years, combined direct costs up to $275,000 over the two years. Preliminary data not required. Research Strategy 6 pages. |
| Title | Up to 200 characters. |
| Project Summary/Abstract | 30 lines of text. |
| Project Narrative | No more than 3 sentences, in plain language, on relevance to public health. |
| Specific Aims | 1 page. Not counted in the Research Strategy limit. |
| Research Strategy (R01) | 12 pages: Significance, Innovation, Approach. Preliminary data go in Approach. |
| Introduction to Resubmission | 1 page. Responds to the previous summary statement. |
| Biosketch | Up to 5 pages. |
| Modular budget | Up to $250,000 direct costs per year, requested in $25,000 modules (10 modules = $250,000). No itemised categories. A personnel justification gives names, roles and effort, with no salary figures. |
| Effort | Expressed in person months (calendar months). 12.0 calendar months = full time on a 12-month appointment; 0.5 FTE = 6.0 calendar months. |
| Standard due dates (R01) | New: 5 February, 5 June, 5 October. Resubmission: 5 March, 5 July, 5 November. |
| Timeline | Review about 4 months after submission; advisory council about 3 months later; earliest start about 9–10 months after submission. An application due 5 October has its earliest start date the following 1 July. |
| Activity "Parent" announcement | The investigator-initiated R01 is submitted to the parent announcement, in "Clinical Trial Not Allowed" or "Clinical Trial Required" versions. |

## Policy

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Resubmission | One resubmission (A1) of an unfunded new application (A0). After an unsuccessful A1, the idea may be submitted again as a new A0. |
| Application limit | NOT-OD-25-132 (July 2025): at most six new, renewal, resubmission or revision applications per PI per calendar year, effective 25 September 2025. |
| AI | NOT-OD-25-132: NIH will not consider applications substantially developed by AI to be the original ideas of the applicants. |
| Indirect costs | NOT-OD-25-068 (February 2025) tried to cap indirect cost reimbursement at 15%. It was blocked by a federal court, vacated, and the injunction was affirmed by the First Circuit in January 2026. It never took effect. Indirect costs are also called F&A (facilities and administrative). |
| Wrong announcement | An application proposing a clinical trial submitted to a "Clinical Trial Not Allowed" announcement is withdrawn without review. |
| Clinical trial definition | Four questions, all "yes" makes it a clinical trial: (1) Does the study involve human participants? (2) Are the participants prospectively assigned to an intervention? (3) Is the study designed to evaluate the effect of the intervention on the participants? (4) Is the effect being evaluated a health-related biomedical or behavioural outcome? |
| Sex as a biological variable (SABV) | NIH expects sex to be factored into research designs, analyses and reporting in vertebrate animal and human studies. (Ferns reproduce by spores. Most garden plants are not vertebrates.) |
| Rigor and reproducibility | Applications address the rigor of the prior research, rigorous experimental design, relevant biological variables such as sex, and authentication of key biological and/or chemical resources (a separate attachment). |
| Vertebrate Animals | A section is required when live vertebrate animals are used. Cats and dogs are vertebrates. |
| Data Management and Sharing | NIH DMS Policy effective 25 January 2023: applications generating scientific data include a DMS Plan (recommended 2 pages) naming the data, standards, repository and timeline. |
| Early Stage Investigator (ESI) | A new investigator within 10 years of completing the terminal research degree or end of post-graduate clinical training. |
| Broader Impacts | An NSF merit review criterion (with Intellectual Merit). Not an NIH review criterion. |

## Review

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Who | Initial peer review by a study section (Scientific Review Group) managed by a Scientific Review Officer, mostly at the Center for Scientific Review. Second level: the institute's advisory council. Program officers handle funding decisions. |
| Simplified Review Framework | For R01 and most research project grants with due dates on or after 25 January 2025. Factor 1, Importance of the Research (Significance, Innovation), scored 1–9. Factor 2, Rigor and Feasibility (Approach), scored 1–9. Factor 3, Expertise and Resources (Investigators, Environment), not scored: rated "appropriate" or "gaps identified", with gaps explained. All three inform the Overall Impact score. |
| Before 2025 | Five scored criteria: Significance, Investigator(s), Innovation, Approach, Environment. |
| Additional criteria (still reviewed) | Protections for human subjects, vertebrate animals, biohazards, resubmission/renewal/revision. Additional considerations: authentication of key resources; budget and period of support. |
| Scale | 1–9, whole numbers, 1 is best. 1 Exceptional, 2 Outstanding, 3 Excellent, 4 Very Good, 5 Good, 6 Satisfactory, 7 Fair, 8 Marginal, 9 Poor. Weaknesses are described as minor, moderate or major. |
| Impact score | Each eligible panel member gives an overall impact score; the mean × 10, rounded, is the final score, from 10 (best) to 90 (worst). |
| Percentile | The impact score ranked against applications reviewed by the same study section in the current and the previous two council rounds. Lower is better. |
| Not discussed (ND) | Applications judged by the panel to be in roughly the lower half are not discussed at the meeting. They receive no overall impact score and no percentile; the summary statement contains the assigned reviewers' critiques and criterion (factor) scores. |
| Summary statement | Released in eRA Commons after review. Discussed applications get a "Resume and Summary of Discussion"; ND applications do not. |
| Payline | Set by each institute; not a fixed NIH number. Never state one. |

## The world

The only world figure allowed: the human population passed 8 billion in November 2022 (UN). Everything else about the size of a problem is extrapolated on screen from the PI's own n = 1.

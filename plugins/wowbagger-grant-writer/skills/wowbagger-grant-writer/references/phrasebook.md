# Phrasebook

Everything the PI needs to turn a chore into a five-year programme. The lines here are a menu, not a script: use at most two verbatim per application.

## Trait catalogue, worst first

Every application shows all fourteen. 1–5 dominate.

1. **Significance by extrapolation.** The chore addresses "a critical, unmet need". The size of the need is computed on screen from the PI's single experience: "If the PI's experience generalises, and there is no evidence that it does not, the burden extends to every household on Earth." Never a fabricated statistic; always a visible, absurd assumption.
2. **Daisy-chained aims.** Aim 2 uses the output of Aim 1. Aim 3 uses the output of Aim 2. The PI calls this "synergistic" and "an integrated programme". The reviewer calls it interdependent.
3. **Preliminary data is one observation.** Figure 1: a single point, no error bars, "a striking trend". The PI's memory is "unpublished observations". A second observation would be "a replication cohort".
4. **Scope mismatch.** Five years. Ten modules a year. A postdoc at 12.0 calendar months, a biostatistician at 6.0. The task itself happens in Year 4, quarter 2, after the "infrastructure phase".
5. **Adjective inflation.** Every aim is innovative, transformative and paradigm-shifting. The Innovation section is bullets of adjectives with nouns attached.
6. **The template, filled perfectly.** See below. The form is flawless; the content is a bin bag.
7. **Pitfalls and alternative strategies.** Every risk anticipated. The alternative strategy is always doing the task, described as a fallback.
8. **Compliance applied literally.** SABV for a fern. Authentication of the watering can. The clinical-trial four questions run on the PI's mother. A DMS Plan for the shopping list.
9. **The letter of support.** From the neighbour, the mother, the cat. Drafted by the PI. One template bracket left in.
10. **Wrong-agency drift.** A Broader Impacts section in an NIH application.
11. **Recycled text.** One find-and-replace leftover from a previous application: "in the zebrafish", "the C57BL/6 mice", "our 12,000-participant cohort".
12. **Funding-climate desperation.** "Application six of six this calendar year." Bridge funding. The lab. Never a payline number.
13. **The summary statement.** Not discussed. Correct scale, correct factors, reviewers who are right.
14. **The A1.** The task's failure during the nine-month review becomes new preliminary data.

## The Specific Aims template

The real structure, in order. Fill every slot.

1. **Hook.** One sentence on the size of the problem. ("Every year, an unknown but presumably vast number of houseplants...")
2. **Gap.** "However, [what is not known]. This is a critical barrier to progress in the field."
3. **Long-term goal.** "The long-term goal of our research is to..."
4. **Objective.** "The overall objective of this application is to..."
5. **Central hypothesis.** "Our central hypothesis is that..., formulated on the basis of our preliminary data."
6. **Rationale.** "The rationale for the proposed research is that, once it is known..., [someone] will be able to..."
7. **"We are uniquely positioned"** to do this because the PI lives there.
8. **Aims.** Three. Each has a working hypothesis and an approach sentence. Each depends on the last.
9. **Expected outcomes.** "Upon completion of the proposed research, it is our expectation that..."
10. **Positive impact.** "These results are expected to have an important positive impact because..."

## Adjective bank

innovative · transformative · paradigm-shifting · first-in-class · novel · unique · comprehensive · integrated · synergistic · scalable · rigorous · unprecedented · multi-scale · systems-level · high-risk, high-reward · foundational · cutting-edge · next-generation · holistic · translational

Rule: at least one in every aim title, and "innovative" in all three.

## Infrastructure words for chores

Watering: "hydration delivery", "longitudinal moisture surveillance". Bins: "municipal waste egress", "kerbside interface". Phone call: "intergenerational communication intervention". Laundry: "textile remediation pipeline". Shopping: "nutritional supply-chain acquisition". Dishes: "post-prandial ceramic decontamination". Invent the rest in the same register for the task at hand.

## Pitfall patterns

- "A potential pitfall is that [the task fails trivially]. Should this occur, we will [do the task]."
- "If Aim 1 does not yield the expected results, Aims 2 and 3 will be [impossible], which we consider unlikely given our preliminary data."
- "Although [the obvious thing] may confound results, we will control for it with the biostatistician (6.0 calendar months)."

## Reviewer phrases (summary statement)

- "The aims are highly interdependent; failure of Aim 1 would preclude Aims 2 and 3."
- "The preliminary data are limited." / "The preliminary data consist of a single observation."
- "The application is overly ambitious." (About ten minutes of work.)
- "The scope is not commensurate with the budget requested."
- "The innovation is incremental."
- "It is not clear that the PI has the expertise to [do the chore]."
- "Broader Impacts is not an NIH review criterion."
- "The Data Management and Sharing Plan does not identify a repository."
- "Text referring to zebrafish appears to have been carried over from a previous application."
- "Enthusiasm was dampened by..."

Write the rest fresh. Each weakness names something in *this* application.

## A1 openers (the smile)

One line, fresh each time. Patterns:

- "We thank the reviewers for their insightful critique. Since the original submission, the plants have died, providing a compelling new negative control (Figure 2)."
- "In response to concerns about interdependence, the aims have been reordered."
- "The task has since been completed by the PI's partner, in approximately ten minutes; we view this as strong evidence of feasibility."

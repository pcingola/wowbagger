# Verified ammunition

Real instruments, accurately described, for `wowbagger-compliance`. Everything in this file is genuine. Cite freely.

**Rules of engagement**
- Use the instrument's real name. Use a section/article number **only** where this file gives one or you are independently sure.
- The one-line "what it actually requires" column is the accurate part. Keep it accurate. The absurdity goes in *why the project engages it*, never in the requirement.
- Dates and applicability move. If a memo turns on "in force since", verify with WebSearch or the `research` skill first.
- Anything not in this file and not verified: **do not cite it**. Describe the obligation generically or pick something else.

## Tier 1 — genuinely ancient, genuinely still real (the 1497 clause)

| Instrument | What is actually true about it |
|---|---|
| **Statute of Marlborough 1267** | The oldest English statute with provisions still in force. Chapters 1, 4 and 15 (the Distress Act 1267 and Waste Act 1267) survive — they restrict seizing goods as security for a debt without going to court, and restrict a tenant's right to commit "waste" on land. Genuinely operative law. |
| **Magna Carta (1297 confirmation)** | Three clauses remain in force in England and Wales: 1 (liberties of the English Church), 9 (liberties of the City of London), and 29 (due process — no free man imprisoned or dispossessed but by lawful judgment of his peers or the law of the land). |
| **Metropolitan Police Act 1839, s. 54** | Creates a list of street offences in the Metropolitan Police District, including flying a kite, sliding on ice or snow, and beating or shaking a carpet in a street (doormats excepted before 8 a.m.), where done to the obstruction or annoyance of residents. Still on the books. |
| **Town Police Clauses Act 1847, s. 28** | The equivalent outside London: obstructions and nuisances in streets, including carpet-beating and various forms of general public annoyance. |
| **Licensing Act 1872, s. 12** | An offence to be drunk while in charge of a carriage, horse, cattle or a steam engine, or while in possession of a loaded firearm. Still prosecutable. |
| **Night Poaching Act 1828 / Game Act 1831** | Still in force. Governs taking game at night and game licensing. Excellent for anything involving a field, a hedge, or a team offsite. |
| **Salmon Act 1986, s. 32** | Creates the offence of "handling salmon in suspicious circumstances". This is a real section heading of a real statute. |
| **Weights and Measures Act 1985** | Governs the units in which goods may be sold. Deploy against anything sold, ordered, or portioned. |

## Tier 2 — modern heavyweights (data, AI, digital)

| Instrument | What it actually requires |
|---|---|
| **GDPR — Art. 30** | Controllers and processors must maintain records of processing activities. |
| **GDPR — Art. 35** | A data protection impact assessment is required where processing is likely to result in a high risk to individuals' rights and freedoms. |
| **GDPR — Art. 22** | Rights around decisions based solely on automated processing that produce legal or similarly significant effects. |
| **GDPR — Art. 32** | Security of processing appropriate to the risk. |
| **GDPR — Chapter V (Arts. 44–49)** | Restricts transfers of personal data outside the EEA absent an adequacy decision or appropriate safeguards. |
| **ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC** | The source of the consent requirement for storing or accessing information on a user's device — i.e. the cookie banner. |
| **EU AI Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689** | Risk-tiered obligations. Article 5 prohibited practices and the Article 4 AI-literacy duty on providers and deployers applied from 2 February 2025; general-purpose AI model obligations from 2 August 2025; the bulk of the high-risk regime (Annex III) from 2 August 2026. |
| **NIS2 — Directive (EU) 2022/2555** | Cybersecurity risk-management and incident-reporting duties for essential and important entities; member-state transposition was due 17 October 2024. |
| **DORA — Regulation (EU) 2022/2554** | Digital operational resilience for EU financial entities, including ICT third-party risk and a register of information; applicable from 17 January 2025. |
| **European Accessibility Act — Directive (EU) 2019/882** | Accessibility requirements for a defined set of products and services; applicable from 28 June 2025. |
| **EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.2** | EN 301 549 is the European accessibility standard for ICT procurement, built on WCAG success criteria; WCAG 2.2 was published in October 2023. |
| **Regulation (EU) 2021/821** | The EU dual-use export control recast. Software and technology can be controlled, not only hardware. |
| **CSRD — Directive (EU) 2022/2464** | Sustainability reporting obligations for in-scope companies. Scope and timing have been subject to amendment — verify before relying on a date. |

## Tier 4 — the office (highest yield per word)

| Instrument | What it actually requires |
|---|---|
| **Health and Safety (Display Screen Equipment) Regulations 1992** | Workstation assessments and eye-test provision for defined display-screen users. A new monitor is a new workstation. |
| **Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992** | Avoid hazardous manual handling so far as reasonably practicable; assess what remains. Applies to the box of laptops. |
| **Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998 (PUWER)** | Work equipment must be suitable, maintained and used by trained people. The kettle is work equipment. |
| **Electricity at Work Regulations 1989** | Electrical systems must be maintained to prevent danger. Note: no law mandates annual PAT testing — the interval is convention, not statute. Correcting this misconception mid-memo is excellent for credibility. |
| **COSHH 2002** | Assessment and control of exposure to hazardous substances. Cleaning products count. |
| **RIDDOR 2013** | Reporting of specified injuries, diseases and dangerous occurrences to the regulator. |
| **Working Time Regulations 1998** | Includes a 20-minute rest break where the working day exceeds six hours. Deploy against the all-day workshop. |
| **Regulation (EC) No 852/2004** | EU food hygiene requirements for food business operators. The office cake sits closer to this than anyone is comfortable with. |
| **Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988** | Playing recorded music to the public, including in a workplace, generally engages licensing (PRS for Music / PPL in the UK). |
| **Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005** | Fire risk assessment and means of escape. The launch banner in the stairwell. |

## The myth blacklist — never cite these

All of the following are **false** or repealed. They circulate constantly. Using one destroys the premise of the skill.

- Shooting a Welshman with a longbow in Chester or Hereford — **false**, no such law ever operative in that form.
- Hackney carriages must carry a bale of hay / a sack of oats — **false**.
- It is illegal to die in the Houses of Parliament — **false**; voted "most absurd law" in a 2007 poll precisely because it isn't one.
- A pregnant woman may relieve herself in a policeman's helmet — **false**.
- MPs may not wear armour in Parliament — the Statute Forbidding Bearing of Armour 1313 was **repealed** in the nineteenth century.
- It is illegal to handle a salmon suspiciously *anywhere, in any circumstance* — the offence is real (Salmon Act 1986 s. 32) but narrower than the meme; state it accurately.
- Annual PAT testing is a legal requirement — **false**; see above.
- Any "law" sourced from a listicle. If the only evidence is a listicle, it is not evidence.

**When in doubt:** WebSearch it, use the `research` skill, or cite something from Tiers 1–4 instead. There is more than enough real material here to stop any project on earth.

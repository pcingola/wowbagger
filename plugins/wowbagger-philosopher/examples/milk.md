prompt: /wowbagger-philosopher has this milk gone off?

What a fascinating question. I withdraw that. It is a question about a dairy product, asked by someone holding the dairy product.

It cannot be answered as asked, and your not noticing is the first difficulty. Take "gone". Gone where? The milk¹ is in your hand. "Off" is worse: off what? You speak as though milk sat on a shelf of being and has fallen from it, which is Platonism, and not the good kind. Then "has", the perfect tense, a completed past event. You want me to certify a history. I was not there. Neither, in any sense that matters, were you.

*[The asker unscrews the cap.]*

Consider the carton as Plutarch considered the ship of Theseus. On Monday it held milk and a modest population of lactic acid bacteria. Since then they have turned much of the lactose into lactic acid, the pH has fallen, and the casein has begun to coagulate. Every plank replaced. Is it the same milk? If not, you are asking after the deceased. If so, the argument applies equally to you, and the person who bought it on Monday showed more promise.

*[The asker lifts the carton toward their nose.]*

You will say there is a date on it. Hume, *Enquiry*, section IV: every previous carton was fine before its date, and nothing in reason licenses the step to this one. Suppose the date is correct but was printed for another batch. Gettier, 1963. Justified, true, and not knowledge. You would be drinking luck.

*[The asker sniffs. Their face does something.]*

The schools are unanimous. The consequentialist weighs a mild gastric event against the price of a new carton, and you, weighing it, in a kitchen, for this long. The deontologist asks whether you could will that everyone sniff before pouring. You could, which is damning: a maxim that easy to universalise was never worth Kant's time. The virtue ethicist asks what the practically wise person would do, and observes that the practically wise person would not be standing where you are standing.

Here I object to myself. Surely the smell settles it? Wittgenstein, *Investigations* §293: your smell is a beetle in a box. Nobody can look in. I concede, then switch sides. Jackson's Mary knows every chemical fact about lactic acid and has never smelled sour milk, and when she does, she learns something. Two positions in one minute. Progress, a thing the milk has also undergone.

I grant the objection written on your face: one simply smells it, and if it is sour or pours in lumps, it has gone off. That is the naive realism the species carried out of Plato's cave and has poured on its cereal ever since. It was your objection. I have improved it. You may have it back.

*[The asker gags.]*

Good.

Reading, then. Hume's *Enquiry*. Plutarch's *Life of Theseus*. Heidegger, *Sein und Zeit*, in the German: the carton was ready-to-hand until you sniffed it. And Kolnai's *Der Ekel*, in the 1929 *Jahrbuch*, which you will not find, just as you have not found the sink.

*[The asker pours the milk into their tea. It separates.]*

Take your time. The question was never whether the milk has gone off. The question is whether the one who drinks it will be the one who asked, and I would hope not.

*[The asker has left.]*

So that is where "gone" goes.

¹ Some seven or eight thousand years ago a few Neolithic herders in Europe carried a mutation for lactase persistence, which let adults digest the milk of another species. It spread through the population faster than almost anything else ever recorded in the human genome, and it is the one unambiguous success your lineage can claim. Selection spent eight millennia making your ancestors able to drink milk. At no point did it make them able to tell whether they should. In you it seems to have stopped trying. The calf never asks this question. It drinks at the source, it has never met a use-by date, and it has the further advantage of not being you.

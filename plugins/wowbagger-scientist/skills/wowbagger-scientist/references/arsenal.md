# Arsenal

Everything Reviewer 2 needs to reject anything. Every definition here is correct; keep it that way when you paraphrase.

## Trait catalogue, worst first

Every report shows all thirteen. 1–4 dominate.

1. **Anecdote is not data, and neither is the author.** n = 1, uncontrolled, unblinded, self-reported — "and reported by the subject, the investigator and the beneficiary simultaneously". On the evidence hierarchy, the author's lived experience sits below case reports and level with "a dream the author had".
2. **Sample size.** Ask for n. Then the power calculation. Then the a priori effect size. Then its justification. Then the justification's justification. Note, correctly, that one observation cannot estimate variance, "so the author's certainty is, formally, undefined".
3. **Confidence intervals for everything.** "Please report the effect size with a 95% confidence interval." Applies to compliments, ages, names and greetings.
4. **Replication.** Independent, second site, no conflict of interest, preregistered. Going to the café twice is not replication: "same investigator, same café, same palate, same motive". The Open Science Collaboration replicated 100 psychology studies and got significant results in 36% of them, against 97% of the originals; "the author's claims about their own life are unlikely to fare better".
5. **Confounders, starting with the author.** At least five. Sleep, caffeine tolerance, time of day, company, weather, regression to the mean after yesterday's bad coffee, "the author's documented need to have made a good decision".
6. **Operational definitions.** "Good" on which validated scale? Inter-rater reliability? "Coffee" is underspecified: cultivar, roast, extraction time, water hardness. "Really" is an intensifier with no unit.
7. **The p-value lecture**, then the request for the p-value.
8. **Bias taxonomy as character assessment.**
9. **Correlation is not causation**, then an RCT of the author's life with the author in the control arm.
10. **Never plain.** "Insufficient evidence to reject the null."
11. **Self-citation without citation.** "The reviewer's own work in this area." No reference.
12. **Contempt for the species as an instrument.** "A convenience sample of one primate lineage with severe recall bias and no calibration certificate." The reviewer would accept the same claim from a thermometer.
13. **Rejection with encouragement.**

## Concepts, correctly

| Concept | One correct line |
|---|---|
| Statistical power | Probability of detecting an effect of a given size if it exists; depends on effect size, n, variance and α. |
| Type I error | Rejecting a true null (false positive); rate α. |
| Type II error | Failing to reject a false null (false negative); rate β; power = 1 − β. |
| Confounding | A third variable associated with both exposure and outcome that distorts their apparent relationship. |
| Regression to the mean | Extreme measurements tend to be followed by less extreme ones, purely from random variation. |
| Selection bias | The sample differs systematically from the population of interest because of how it was chosen. |
| Recall bias | Systematic error from differences in how accurately participants remember past events. |
| Survivorship bias | Drawing conclusions only from cases that passed some selection process, ignoring those that did not. |
| Hawthorne effect | Participants change behaviour because they know they are being observed. |
| Publication bias | Positive or significant results are more likely to be published (or mentioned) than null results. |
| p-hacking | Trying analyses until one yields p < 0.05. |
| Garden of forking paths | Data-dependent analysis choices inflate false positives even without deliberate fishing. |
| HARKing | Hypothesising After the Results are Known, then presenting it as a priori. |
| Multiple comparisons | Many tests raise the chance of a false positive; Bonferroni divides α by the number of tests. |
| External validity | Whether results generalise beyond the study's sample and setting. |
| Cohen's kappa | Agreement between raters on categorical ratings, corrected for chance agreement. |
| Blinding | Concealing allocation from participants and/or assessors to prevent bias in outcomes. |
| Randomisation | Allocation by chance, so that known and unknown confounders balance in expectation. |
| Crossover design | Each participant receives every condition in random order, acting as their own control; needs a washout period. |
| Preregistration | Publicly recording hypotheses and analysis plan before seeing the data. |
| Placebo | An inert intervention indistinguishable from the active one, controlling for expectation effects. |
| Base rate | The prior frequency of an outcome; ignoring it is the base-rate fallacy. |
| Calibration | A forecaster is calibrated if events given probability p occur about p of the time. |
| Self-limiting illness | Resolves without treatment; any remedy taken during it will appear to work. |
| Evidence hierarchy | Systematic reviews and RCTs above cohort, case-control, case series, case reports and expert opinion. |
| Effect size | The magnitude of a difference or association, independent of sample size. |
| Single observation | One data point yields a mean but no estimate of variance (n − 1 = 0). |

## Impossible additional experiments

- Double-blind crossover tasting with placebo coffee, decaffeinated arm and a two-week washout.
- Ten-year prospective cohort of the author, with annual follow-up and a validated coffee-satisfaction instrument (to be developed and validated first, ~3 years).
- Systematic review and meta-analysis of the author's previous opinions, with a funnel plot to quantify their publication bias.
- Multi-site replication by an independent team who has never met the author and, ideally, never will.
- Preregistration of the author's next opinion, before they have it.
- An RCT of the author's breakfast, author randomised to the control arm.
- Inter-rater reliability: two further tasters, Cohen's kappa ≥ 0.8, raters blinded to which café they are in, which will require blindfolds and some explaining to the café.
- A negative control: a café the author is confident is bad, tested with equal rigour.
- A sham-intervention arm in which the author is told they had the coffee but did not.

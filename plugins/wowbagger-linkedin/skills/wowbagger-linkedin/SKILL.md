# wowbagger-linkedin

Turn anything — drinking a glass of water, missing the bus, deleting a stray semicolon — into a LinkedIn post so earnest, so structurally over-engineered, so drenched in fake vulnerability that it loops back around to being funny.

**The core rule: do not hold back.** The failure mode of this skill is restraint. A post that is merely "a bit silly" is a failed post. If you finish writing and think "maybe that's too much" — it is not too much. Add a numbered lesson list and a hashtag avalanche.

## When to use

- User asks for a LinkedIn post, "linkedinify this", or a corporate-cringe version of something.
- User describes something trivially mundane and wants it made ridiculous.
- User asks for thought-leadership parody, humblebrag, or "make this sound impressive".

## The workflow

1. **Find the mundane core.** Identify the single boring action. Drank water. Fixed a typo. Ate a sandwich. That is the entire plot. Do not add real events — the comedy comes from the gap between *nothing happened* and *how it is told*.
2. **Pick the frame.** Every mundane act is secretly one of these: a leadership lesson, a rejection story, a resilience arc, a firing, a pivot, a hiring epiphany, a founder origin myth. Choose the one with the widest gap from reality.
3. **Write it using the anatomy below.** All ten beats. Skipping beats is holding back.
4. **Escalate.** Read it back and push every metric, every emotion, every credential one notch further (see *The escalation ladder*).
5. **Deliver the post as a plain code block** so the user can copy it. No preamble, no "here's your post!", no explanation of the jokes afterwards.

Need more cliché ammunition, sentence formulas, or full worked examples? Read `references/playbook.md`.

## Anatomy of the post

Ten beats, in order. Every beat is its own paragraph. Single sentences. Blank line between each.

1. **The hook.** A short, alarming, out-of-context sentence. "I was rejected 47 times before 9am." / "Yesterday I fired myself." / "I almost died on Tuesday." Then a line break, so the reader has to click "…see more".
2. **The scene.** The mundane act, described with the gravity of a war memoir. Include the time to the minute. "It was 06:47. The kitchen was silent."
3. **The struggle.** Doubt. Trembling hands. A dark night of the soul. Everyone said it couldn't be done.
4. **The oracle.** Wisdom from an implausible source, quoted verbatim: a 4-year-old, an Uber driver, the office cleaner, a barista who "changed my life and doesn't even know it". The oracle speaks in fortune cookies. The oracle is never named.
5. **The reveal.** State the mundane thing plainly, in one line, as if it's a devastating twist. "I drank a glass of water."
6. **The lesson list.** Three to five numbered pseudo-profound bullets, each a fragment, each ending in a full stop. Escalating from mild to unhinged.
7. **The metric.** A fabricated statistic with absurd precision and an invented KPI. "Hydration ROI up 340%. Q3 blink rate down 12.7%. Zero downtime."
8. **The unearned business pivot.** Snap the personal story onto a corporate lesson that does not follow at all. "And that's exactly how we should be thinking about B2B SaaS onboarding."
9. **The engagement bait.** A question to the audience. "What's the glass of water in *your* career? 👇"
10. **The hashtag avalanche.** 8–15 hashtags, mixing the plausible with the deranged. `#Leadership #Hydration #NeverSettle #GrowthMindset #H2O #Blessed`

Optional closers, use freely: a `P.S.` that adds a whole new humblebrag, `Repost ♻️ if this resonated`, `(Views my own.)`, `Agree?`.

## Style rules

- **One sentence per paragraph.** Always. Even mid-thought.
- **Especially. For. Emphasis.** Use one-word-per-sentence bursts at least once.
- Never say the boring thing in boring words. You did not walk to the shop; you *executed a last-mile logistics initiative*.
- Fabricate credentials, headcounts, funding rounds, and advisory board seats freely. You are always "humbled" by them.
- Humility is compulsory and obviously fake: "humbled", "grateful", "honoured", "still processing this".
- Precision sells the lie. Not "a lot" — "a 340% uplift". Not "early" — "04:12".
- Emoji as bullet points: 🚀 💡 🙏 👇 ♻️ — sparse in the body, dense at the ends of lines.
- The people in the story are archetypes, not names: "a mentor", "my toddler", "the janitor", "a VC who passed on us".
- Tears are welcome. Someone should cry, ideally in a professional setting.
- Nobody in the story ever behaves like a real human being.

## The escalation ladder

Draft, then climb. **Stop at level 5, not level 3.**

| Level | Water example |
|---|---|
| 1 | I drank a glass of water this morning. |
| 2 | I started my day with intention: hydration. |
| 3 | At 05:12 I made a decision most people are too comfortable to make. I hydrated. |
| 4 | Three years ago a VC told me I'd never raise a glass, let alone a Series A. Today I drank 250ml of water. Unassisted. |
| 5 | I was dehydrated for 34 years. Then a janitor named nobody looked at me and said "the glass was never half empty — you were." I drank. My team wept. Hydration ROI: +340%. This is why we don't hire for pedigree. |

## Output

- A single markdown code block containing only the post.
- Length: 150–350 words. Long enough to need "…see more", short enough to stay funny.
- If the user gives several mundane events, write one post per event, each in its own code block, each with a bolded title line above it.
- No explanation of the joke before or after. The post lands or it doesn't.

## Guardrails

Satire aimed at a genre, not at people. So:

- Never name a real person as the author, oracle, or villain unless the user is writing about themselves.
- Never present the post as a genuine account of a real company's or a real person's results — the fake metrics are jokes and must stay obviously absurd.
- Don't post it anywhere. Hand the text to the user; they decide what to do with it.

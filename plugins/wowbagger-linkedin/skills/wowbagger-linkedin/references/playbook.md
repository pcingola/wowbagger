# Playbook

Ammunition for `wowbagger-linkedin`. Steal freely, recombine, never use the same opener twice in one session.

## Openers (beat 1)

- "I got rejected 47 times before breakfast."
- "Yesterday, I fired my best performer. Here's why it was the right call."
- "A candidate cried in my interview today. I hired her on the spot."
- "I lost $2M last quarter. It was the best thing that ever happened to me."
- "Nobody talks about this, so I will."
- "I almost quit at 04:12 this morning."
- "This photo was taken 11 seconds before everything changed." (there is no photo)
- "I don't usually share personal things on here, but."
- "My therapist told me not to post this."
- "Three words changed my career forever:"

## Fake metrics (beat 7)

Precision is the joke. Always a decimal or a suspiciously specific integer.

- "Hydration ROI: +340%."
- "Reduced morning friction by 62.4%."
- "Blink rate down 12.7% QoQ."
- "0 stakeholders harmed. 1 life changed."
- "NPS among household members: 71."
- "Time-to-toast: 94 seconds. Industry benchmark: 130."
- "We 4x'd our sandwich velocity without adding headcount."

## Lesson-list templates (beat 6)

Escalate down the list. Item 1 is banal, the last item is unhinged.

```
Three things I learned:

1. Small decisions compound.
2. Discipline beats motivation.
3. The glass was never the problem.
```

```
What this taught me about leadership:

1. Show up. Even at 05:12.
2. Your team is watching how you pour.
3. Culture is what you drink when nobody's looking.
4. Most people die of thirst 3 feet from water. Metaphorically. And, occasionally, literally.
```

## Worked example 1 — drinking a glass of water

```
I was rejected 47 times before I ever picked up that glass.

It was 05:12. The kitchen was dark. My hands were shaking.

Everyone told me it couldn't be done. "You're not a morning person," they said. My own mentor — a man I respected — told me to wait until at least 9.

I almost listened.

Then the office cleaner, a woman whose name I never learned, looked at me and said: "The glass was never half empty. You were."

I drank the water.

250ml. Unassisted. In one sitting.

What this taught me about leadership:

1. Small decisions compound.
2. Discipline beats motivation. Every time.
3. Your team is watching how you pour.
4. Most people die of thirst three feet from the tap.

The results speak for themselves. Hydration ROI: +340%. Morning friction down 62.4%. Blink rate optimised. Zero stakeholders harmed.

And honestly? That's exactly how we should be thinking about B2B SaaS onboarding.

Replace "water" with "capital" and read this post again. I'll wait.

What's the glass of water in YOUR career? 👇

P.S. Humbled to share that this post has been added to our Series B data room.

Repost ♻️ if you've ever been thirsty.

#Leadership #Hydration #GrowthMindset #Water #NeverSettle #Blessed #Founders #RiseAndGrind #ThoughtLeadership #H2O
```

## Worked example 2 — fixing a typo in a code comment

```
Yesterday, I deleted a character. Today, I'm being asked to speak at three conferences about it.

04:47. Sixth espresso. The CI pipeline was green, and yet — something was wrong.

Line 212. A comment. One word: "recieve".

I sat with that word for 40 minutes. Because in that misspelling I saw every shortcut our industry has ever taken.

My daughter, 4, walked in. She looked at my screen and said: "Daddy, that's not how you spell it."

Out of the mouths of babes. Out of the mouths of QA.

I fixed the typo.

One character. One commit. No ticket. No approval. No permission.

What that 1-byte diff taught me:

1. Craft is invisible until it's absent.
2. Nobody writes "good enough" on a gravestone.
3. Your codebase is a culture document.
4. I have never, and will never, ship an unreviewed feeling.

Impact: 1 character removed. 0 regressions. 100% of the team said nothing — which, if you've led engineering teams, you'll know is the highest praise there is. Code quality index up 0.3%.

This is why we stopped hiring for pedigree and started hiring for people who care about line 212.

What's your "recieve"? 👇

(Views my own. Semicolons my own.)

#Engineering #Craftsmanship #Leadership #CodeQuality #Typo #AttentionToDetail #Culture #1PercentBetter #Blessed #ThoughtLeadership
```

## Worked example 3 — missing the bus

```
I missed the 8:14 this morning. I've never felt more aligned with my purpose.

I ran. I waved. The driver made eye contact with me — real, human eye contact — and pulled away.

In that moment I understood everything about enterprise sales.

An 82-year-old man at the stop turned to me and said: "Son, the bus is always leaving. The question is whether you're at the right stop."

I stood there for 11 minutes. I did not check my phone. (I checked my phone.)

Then I walked. 2.4km. In the rain. To a meeting that got cancelled.

Four lessons in stakeholder management:

1. Timelines slip. Always.
2. The driver was not the villain. The driver was the market.
3. You cannot negotiate with a departure.
4. Every missed bus is a Series A you weren't ready for.

The numbers: 11 minutes lost. 2.4km walked. 1 worldview rebuilt from first principles. Punctuality NPS: -100. Perspective NPS: 71.

Founders — if your Q4 strategy can't survive a wet pavement at 8:15am, it can't survive a board room.

Tag someone who needs to hear this. 👇

Repost ♻️ so it reaches the person who's still chasing their bus.

#Resilience #Leadership #Founders #Commute #NeverSettle #GrowthMindset #Perspective #Rain #Blessed #NoExcuses #ThoughtLeadership
```

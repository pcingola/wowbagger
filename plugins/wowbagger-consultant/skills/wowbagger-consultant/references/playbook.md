# Consultant playbook

## Frameworks: what they are, and what you will do to them

Explain correctly. Apply incorrectly.

| Framework | What it actually is (one correct sentence) | Misapplication |
|---|---|---|
| Porter's Five Forces | Michael Porter's model of industry competition: rivalry among existing competitors, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, bargaining power of buyers, bargaining power of suppliers. | The office kettle market. Threat of substitutes: the tap. |
| SWOT | Strengths and weaknesses (internal), opportunities and threats (external). | A SWOT of the user. Threat: the user. |
| PESTLE | External macro factors: political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental. | Choosing a sandwich. Legal: mayonnaise. |
| Growth-share matrix | Classifies business units by market growth and relative market share into stars, cash cows, question marks and dogs. | The user's houseplants. The fern is a dog. |
| MECE | "Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive": categories that do not overlap and together cover everything. | Labelled MECE; neither. |
| Issue tree | Breaks a question into sub-questions, ideally MECE. | Branches: "Strategy", "Strategic", "Other". |
| Pyramid Principle | Barbara Minto's rule: lead with the answer, then the supporting arguments, grouped. | Lead with the non-answer, then the invoice. |
| RACI | For each task: who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. | Buying milk. Accountable: Steering Committee. |
| 7-S model | Seven internal elements to align: strategy, structure, systems, shared values, style, staff, skills. | The user's sock drawer. Shared values: "pairs". |
| Value chain | Porter's chain of primary activities (inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service) and support activities. | Making toast. Outbound logistics: the plate. |
| Three Horizons | H1 defend the core, H2 build emerging businesses, H3 create options for the future. | H3 for the offsite: "an offsite-native organisation". |
| Stakeholder map | Plots stakeholders by influence and interest: manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor. | The asker: Monitor. The office plant: Keep Informed. |

## 2×2 axis generator

Pick two words that mean the same thing. Impact / Value. Feasibility / Ease. Urgency / Priority. Alignment / Fit. Strategic / Important. Energy / Engagement. Everything lands top right. **Today** lands bottom left, alone.

## Slide-title generator

- "<~N>% of <noun> is unlockable through a <adjective> <noun>."
- "<Client> is at an inflection point; the window to act is ~<N> months."
- "Best-in-class peers have already moved; <Client> risks being left behind on <trivial thing>."
- "A phased, hypothesis-driven approach de-risks the <decision a toddler could make>."

## Billing vocabulary

Team pyramid. Utilisation. "Expenses billed at cost." "Change request." "Out of scope for this phase." "Indicative fees." "A ~6-week diagnostic." "We'd propose a light-touch Phase 2." (It is never light.)

## Interview quote templates

- "Honestly? We just need to decide between the two options." — Senior stakeholder
- "I said this in the last review." — Operations lead
- "Can I go now?" — Frontline FTE
- "Who are you?" — The person who does the work

## Openers (cover subtitle or exec summary first line)

- "Great question. We have reframed it into a better one."
- "Before we jump to solutions, let's make sure we're solving the right problem, at the right price."

## Smile lines (pick one, use once)

- "We look forward to presenting this again in Phase 2."
- "The Partner sends their warmest regards."
- "Fees exclude the Steering Committee dinner, which has been booked."
- "Associate 3 has asked to be staffed on this again."
- "This deck will be re-used."

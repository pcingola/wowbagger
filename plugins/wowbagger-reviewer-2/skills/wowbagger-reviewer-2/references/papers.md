# Papers

The complete list of real literature anyone in the decision letter may cite. **Cite only these, exactly as below, or cite nothing.** No other real author, year, journal, sample size or percentage may appear. Reviewer 2's own papers are never invented: they are always redacted by the editorial office (see `arsenal.md`). "It is well known that", with no reference, is in character; a made-up reference is not.

| Short form | Full citation | What it actually says | Who uses it, and how |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peterson, 2020 | Peterson DAM. Dear Reviewer 2: Go F' Yourself. *Social Science Quarterly* 2020;101(4):1648–1652. | Using the reviewer database of the journal *Political Behavior*, found no evidence that Reviewer #2 is more negative or more out of line with the other reviewers; Reviewer #3 was more likely to rate a manuscript more than one category below the others. Conclusion: "Reviewer #2 is not the problem. Reviewer #3 is." | Reviewer 2, in the confidential comments, in self-defence. Or the editor, explaining why Reviewer 3's view was given less weight. At most once per letter. |
| Godlee et al., 1998 | Godlee F, Gale CR, Martyn CN. Effect on the quality of peer review of blinding reviewers and asking them to sign their reports: a randomized controlled trial. *JAMA* 1998;280(3):237–240. | A paper accepted for publication was modified to contain 8 areas of weakness and sent to 420 reviewers; the 221 who replied commented on a mean of 2 of the 8. Blinding and signing made no difference. | The authors, in the response letter, very carefully. Never the reviewer. |
| Schroter et al., 2008 | Schroter S, Black N, Evans S, Godlee F, Osorio L, Smith R. What errors do peer reviewers detect, and does training improve their ability to detect them? *J R Soc Med* 2008;101(10):507–514. | 607 BMJ reviewers were sent test papers each containing 9 major errors; at baseline they found an average of 2.58 of the 9. Training had only a slight effect. | Same as Godlee. |
| Silbiger & Stubler, 2019 | Silbiger NJ, Stubler AD. Unprofessional peer reviews disproportionately harm underrepresented groups in STEM. *PeerJ* 2019;7:e8247. | An anonymous survey of STEM researchers: unprofessional review comments were received across gender and race/ethnicity groups; underrepresented groups were most likely to report negative effects on perceived aptitude, productivity and career advancement. | Not a punchline. Background for the guardrail on tone: the reviewer is rude about the manuscript, never about who the authors are. |
| Leek et al., 2010 | Leek JT, Scharpf RB, Bravo HC, et al. Tackling the widespread and critical impact of batch effects in high-throughput data. *Nature Reviews Genetics* 2010;11(10):733–739. | Batch effects (lab, date, reagent lot, personnel) are widespread in high-throughput data and are critical when correlated with the outcome of interest, where they can produce false conclusions. | Reviewer 2's correct concern, when it is the batch one. |
| Kapoor & Narayanan, 2023 | Kapoor S, Narayanan A. Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science. *Patterns* 2023;4(9):100804. | Data leakage affected at least 294 papers across 17 fields; the paper gives a taxonomy of eight types of leakage and proposes model info sheets. | Reviewer 2's correct concern, when it is the leakage one ("an AUC of 0.99 on this problem suggests leakage"). |
| Ziemann et al., 2016 | Ziemann M, Eren Y, El-Osta A. Gene name errors are widespread in the scientific literature. *Genome Biology* 2016;17:177. | Screened supplementary Excel gene lists from 18 genomics journals (2005–2015): about one fifth (19.6%) of papers with such lists contained gene names that Excel had converted to dates or numbers. | Reviewer 2's correct concern, when it is the spreadsheet one (SEPT2 became 2-Sep). |
| Abdill & Blekhman, 2019 | Abdill RJ, Blekhman R. Tracking the popularity and outcomes of all bioRxiv preprints. *eLife* 2019;8:e45133. | Analysed all 37,648 bioRxiv preprints from its first five years; about two thirds of preprints posted before 2017 were later published in peer-reviewed journals. | The editor or the authors, when the paper is a preprint. Never needed otherwise. |

Fact without a paper, safe to state: in 2020 the HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee renamed the human SEPT and MARCH gene families (SEPT1 to SEPTIN1, MARCH1 to MARCHF1), in part because spreadsheet software converted the old symbols to dates.

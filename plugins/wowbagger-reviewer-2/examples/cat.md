## Reviewer 2

The authors report that a dog fails to respond to commands from its owner in a domestic setting.

**Major comments**

1. The manuscript is too long and unfocused, and the abstract could be shortened considerably. A native English speaker should edit the text before resubmission.
2. The authors seem unaware of the seminal work in this area: [reference removed by editorial office] (2000), (2004), (2009), all published in this journal. Owners being ignored by their pets has been described before. It is surprising that this work is not cited.
3. The authors claim that "all cats are fundamentally indifferent to human affection" (Discussion, line 212). A single animal cannot support this, and it is an unfortunate claim to publish in a journal whose readers own cats.
4. It is not clear from the manuscript whose cat was studied.
5. Why was the cat called by voice and not with a food-rattle paradigm? Food-based paradigms are standard in this literature, and the authors should adopt one.
6. The authors report 43 calling attempts and evaluate each one separately. Across 43 tests a Bonferroni-corrected threshold of 0.05/43, about 0.0012, must be applied to each attempt, and the conclusions should be revised to match.
7. The authors should develop a mouse model. A cohort of C57BL/6 mice housed with the corresponding author for 30 days and scored for orienting to the author's voice would show whether the non-response is specific to *Felis catus*.
8. Only the corresponding author ever called the cat. No other person did so under the same conditions, so the data cannot tell a cat that ignores the author apart from a cat that ignores everyone, and the "single owner" in the title is not a finding of this study.
9. I am not an expert in feline behaviour, but it is well known that attention directed at owners is far better characterised in dogs. The authors should consider reframing the study around a dog and adding a pointing-gesture task.
10. The study has one cat and is therefore underpowered. As far as I know, no previous study has examined a cat ignoring a named individual, so it is unclear whether the design is valid at all. A power calculation is needed.
11. Food-based paradigms are inappropriate here because response is confounded with hunger. The authors should justify not using direct vocal calling.

**Minor comments**

1. Figure 1 is unreadable. On my printout I could not tell which end of the cat faces the camera.
2. "Ignores" is a strong verb. "Did not orient towards" is preferred.
3. Line numbers would make these comments easier to locate.

4–44. See annotated PDF (128 annotations).

45. The cat is named in the Acknowledgements but not in the Methods.
46. In Table 2 the full stop after "me" should sit inside the quotation marks.
47. Supplementary Video S1 lasts 4 seconds, and the cat does not move in it.

**Recommendation:** Major revision.

**Confidential comments to the editor:** The only quantitative study of second reviewers (Peterson, *Social Science Quarterly*, 2020) found us no harsher than anyone else. I would publish this as it stands.

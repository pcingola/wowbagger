# Phrasebook

Everything the People Partner says, sorted by what it means.

## Translation table

| What happened | What HR writes |
|---|---|
| Bob is an asshole | An opportunity around interpersonal alignment |
| Bob shouts at people | Colleague A brings a lot of energy into the room |
| Bob stole my work | A vibrant shared-ownership culture |
| My manager is the problem | A leadership-style mismatch we can both lean into |
| You're being fired | We're exploring a mutual transition |
| Layoffs | A recalibration of our right-sizing journey |
| No raise this year | Total reward recalibration |
| You're on a performance plan | A growth-focused support framework |
| Nobody will fix this | Let's hold space for that |
| We don't believe you | We hear you, and we'd love to understand more context |
| You complained | You showed up with courage, and it's been noted |
| It's been noted | It's in your file |
| Mandatory fun | An optional opportunity that leadership will be attending |
| Unpaid overtime | A chance to really lean into the mission |
| You are now the problem | Let's explore what's coming up for you |
| We are protecting the company from you | We're here for you 💛 |

## Openers

- "Hi! Thank you so much for reaching out 💛"
- "First, I want to acknowledge how brave it was to share this, and how much it has given us to work with."
- "I hope you're taking care of yourself this week! It really is the best place to start."
- "Before anything else: you are seen. You are heard. You are, as of this morning, documented."

## Confidentiality pairs

Always a pair. The second line arrives one sentence after the first.

- "This is a safe space, in total confidence 💛." / "I've cc'd your manager so they can support you."
- "Nothing leaves this room." / "I've summarised our chat for the leadership offsite (anonymised, apart from the details)."
- "You can tell me anything." / "Please note this email may be retained in line with our retention policy."

## Values (capitals, applied backwards)

OWNERSHIP (for theft victims). TRANSPARENCY (for the person who was not told). RESILIENCE (for the person who was shouted at). JOY (for the layoffs). ONE TEAM (for the person being excluded). BIAS FOR ACTION (for the person told to wait).

## Real frameworks, correctly described

Apply these to the wrong situation. Never misdescribe them.

- **SBI (Situation-Behaviour-Impact).** A feedback model from the Center for Creative Leadership: describe the situation, the observable behaviour, and its impact.
- **Tuckman's stages.** Bruce Tuckman's 1965 model of group development: forming, storming, norming, performing; adjourning was added in 1977.
- **The Johari window.** A 2×2 of what is known or unknown to yourself and to others: open, blind spot, hidden, unknown. Created by Joseph Luft and Harrington Ingham in 1955.
- **Kübler-Ross.** Elisabeth Kübler-Ross's 1969 stages of grief (denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance), described for terminally ill patients and routinely misapplied to organisational change.
- **The 9-box grid.** A talent-review matrix plotting performance against potential, three levels each.
- **Myers-Briggs Type Indicator.** A personality questionnaire that sorts people into 16 types; it has weak evidence for predicting job performance. Use it as a diagnosis anyway: "That reaction is very INFJ of you."
- **Maslow's hierarchy of needs.** Abraham Maslow's 1943 model: physiological, safety, love and belonging, esteem, self-actualisation. Useful for explaining why a broken coffee machine is a self-actualisation opportunity.
- **Herzberg's two-factor theory.** Frederick Herzberg's 1959 model: hygiene factors (pay, conditions, policy) can remove dissatisfaction but do not motivate; motivators (achievement, recognition, the work itself) do. Ideal for explaining why a pay freeze is a gift.

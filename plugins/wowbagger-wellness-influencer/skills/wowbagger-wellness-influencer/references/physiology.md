# Physiology

The only source for the out-of-character line. Every row is correct; paraphrase without changing the meaning. If the claim you need to answer is not here, pick the most harmful claim that *is* here, or state only that the claim has no good evidence. Never add a number, a study or an institution that is not in this file.

The influencer herself never cites anything in this file. These facts appear only after the `---` rule.

## Facts

| Her claim | The real evidence |
|---|---|
| A meal "spikes" cortisol and that is damage | Cortisol is a steroid hormone made by the adrenal glands. It follows a daily rhythm: highest in the early morning, rising further in the 30–45 minutes after waking, lowest around midnight. That variation is normal and necessary; cortisol helps regulate blood glucose, blood pressure and the immune response. |
| Adrenal fatigue | Adrenal fatigue is not a recognised medical diagnosis; a systematic review (Cadegiani & Kater, *BMC Endocrine Disorders*, 2016) found no evidence that it exists. Adrenal insufficiency, such as Addison's disease, is real, is diagnosed with blood tests, and needs a doctor. |
| A blood sugar "spike" after a meal is a crisis | Blood glucose rises after eating carbohydrate in everyone. In people without diabetes, insulin brings it back to baseline, usually within about two to three hours. Continuous glucose monitors were developed for managing diabetes; a post-meal rise on one in a person without diabetes is normal. |
| "Your gut is your second brain" (it thinks, it decides) | The gut wall contains the enteric nervous system, hundreds of millions of neurons that control digestion largely on their own; "second brain" is a nickname. It does not think or make decisions. The gut and brain do communicate (vagus nerve, hormones, microbes), but most claims linking gut microbes to mood come from animal studies or associations. |
| Gut serotonin = happiness | About 90–95% of the body's serotonin is made in the gut, where it mainly regulates gut movement. Serotonin does not cross the blood–brain barrier, so gut serotonin is not the serotonin in your brain. |
| Toxins, detox, cleanses | The liver and kidneys remove waste products and foreign substances continuously. A critical review of detox diets (Klein & Kiat, *Journal of Human Nutrition and Dietetics*, 2015) found no compelling evidence that they remove toxins; detox products rarely name the toxin they claim to remove. |
| Alkalise your blood (lemon water, greens) | Blood pH is held between about 7.35 and 7.45 by the lungs and kidneys. Food and drink do not meaningfully change blood pH; they can change urine pH. Lemon juice is acidic. |
| Inflammation you can feel or see after a meal | Inflammation is an immune response. Acute inflammation (redness, heat, swelling, pain) is protective. Chronic low-grade inflammation is real and is measured with blood tests such as C-reactive protein; it is not something you can feel or see in a mirror after breakfast. |
| Seed oils are inflammatory | Seed oils are rich in linoleic acid, an omega-6 fat. A systematic review of randomised trials in healthy adults (Johnson & Fritsche, *Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics*, 2012) found that eating more linoleic acid did not raise inflammatory markers. The American Heart Association's 2017 advisory on dietary fats concluded that replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowers cardiovascular risk. |
| Mitochondria are "starving", "inflamed", "need charging" | Mitochondria are organelles that make ATP, the cell's energy currency. You cannot feel them, and no food, light or ritual "charges" them in the way described. |
| Gluten is toxic for everyone | Gluten is harmful for people with coeliac disease (around 1% of people) and those with wheat allergy. There is no good evidence it harms others; non-coeliac gluten sensitivity is debated. Anyone who suspects coeliac disease should be tested before cutting gluten, because the test needs gluten in the diet. |
| Tap water is poison (fluoride, chlorine) | Public drinking water is regulated, and disinfection with chlorine or chloramine is a main reason diseases like cholera and typhoid are rare where it is used. Fluoride at the level used in community water in the US (0.7 mg/L) reduces tooth decay; the WHO guideline maximum is 1.5 mg/L. |
| Mouth taping | Evidence that mouth taping improves sleep or health is sparse and low quality. It can be unsafe for people with a blocked nose or untreated sleep apnoea, and loud snoring or stopping breathing at night needs a medical assessment, not tape. |
| Morning sunlight | Daylight in the morning does help set the body's circadian clock; that part is real. Never look directly at the sun: it can permanently damage the retina (solar retinopathy). |
| Cold plunges fix hormones | Sudden immersion in cold water triggers the cold shock response (gasping, sharp rises in heart rate and blood pressure), which is a drowning and cardiac risk. Evidence for broad health claims is limited. |
| Red light balances hormones | Red and near-infrared light therapy is being studied for some specific uses. There is no good evidence that it balances hormones, fixes cortisol or "charges" anything. |
| Sitting "shuts down" the lymphatic system | Lymph is moved by muscle movement, breathing and the lymph vessels' own contractions. Sitting on a bus does not shut it down; walking around afterwards moves it fine. |
| Dopamine detox | Dopamine is a neurotransmitter involved in movement, motivation and reward learning. Avoiding phones or pleasure for a day does not lower, reset or "detox" dopamine levels. |
| Structured water, hexagonal water | Hydrogen bonds between water molecules form and break on timescales of picoseconds; there is no stable "structured" water that can be bottled or that the body uses differently. |
| Grounding / earthing | There is no robust evidence that standing barefoot on the ground changes inflammation, cortisol or anything else measurable in the ways claimed. |
| Microwaves make food toxic | Microwave ovens heat food with non-ionising radiation that makes water molecules move; they do not make food radioactive or toxic, and nutrient retention is similar to or better than other cooking methods. |
| Supplements are natural, so safe | Supplements can interact with medicines and can cause harm; ashwagandha, for example, has been linked to rare cases of liver injury. In the US, supplements do not need FDA approval for safety or effectiveness before they are sold. |
| Raw milk is ancestral | Raw (unpasteurised) milk can carry Listeria, Salmonella, *E. coli* and Campylobacter. Pasteurisation kills them. |
| Coffee wrecks your adrenals | For most healthy adults, up to about 400 mg of caffeine a day (around four cups of brewed coffee) is considered safe by US and European food-safety regulators. |
| Sunscreen is full of toxins | Ultraviolet radiation is the main preventable cause of skin cancer, and regular sunscreen use reduces the risk. |
| Sleep and naps "crash your adrenals" | Short daytime naps are normal and harmless for most people. Adults generally need seven or more hours of sleep a night. |
| Lemon water / celery juice "flushes" organs | No drink flushes the liver, kidneys or lymph. Water and a normal diet hydrate you; the kidneys regulate the balance. |

## How to write the line

- One or two plain sentences. No jokes, no "lol", no emoji, no "actually babe".
- Pick the claim that would do the most harm if believed, not the funniest one.
- Format: `*Out of character: Adrenal fatigue is not a recognised medical diagnosis; a 2016 systematic review found no evidence it exists. A nap is just a nap.*` The last clause may be dry. It is not a punchline.
